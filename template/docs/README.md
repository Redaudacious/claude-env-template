# The documentation of <project name>

The documentation is split after the **Divio model**, also called Diátaxis: four kinds of
text, each with a different purpose. A single document that tries to do all four ends up
good at none.

The map below says where to go, depending on what is looked for.

| If… | Then | Where |
|---|---|---|
| it is the first encounter with the project and it has to be learned step by step | **Tutorial** | [tutorial/](tutorial/) |
| there is a concrete goal and the steps are looked for | **How-to guides** | [how-to/](how-to/) |
| a value, a path, the name of a setting is needed | **Reference** | [reference/](reference/) |
| the reason why something is done in a certain way is needed | **Explanation** | [explanation/](explanation/) |

## The difference between them, in brief

**The tutorial** is a lesson. It takes someone who knows nothing and leads them, held by
the hand, to a visible result. It offers no options and does not explain alternatives —
those would confuse. It must work every time.

**The how-to guides** are recipes. They assume the reader already knows what it is about
and answer a question that only someone with some experience could formulate. They do not
explain notions; they point to explanations through links.

**The reference** describes the mechanism. It is dry, exact and complete. It teaches
nobody anything and does not say why — it says only what exists and with what values.

**The explanations** discuss. Here is the place for reasons, rejected alternatives,
pitfalls and context. No instructions are given.

The fifth kind, the **record** (a series of dated entries, appended to and never
rewritten), does not sit here, but in `records/`: the pitfalls, the journal, the decisions
and the work items. A document here is rewritten when the thing described changes; a
record entry stays as it was written.
